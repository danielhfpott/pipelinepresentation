# Corti Assignment / Judging Brief — Source-Reported Constraints

## Status

**Source:** user-supplied transcript of Corti's spoken hackathon assignment/judging briefing.

This is substantially stronger than prior memory-based descriptions because it preserves the actual wording as heard by the team. It should still be reconciled against any official written event document if/when supplied.

## 1. Build space described by Corti

The briefing explicitly invited teams to build across multiple moments and boundaries of care, including examples such as:

- assistance during an encounter;
- post-care documentation / task automation;
- patient explanation after diagnosis;
- longitudinal pattern finding across one patient's history;
- pattern finding across multiple patients;
- care handoff between clinicians/shifts;
- connecting conversational data to other systems through MCP or another integration;
- something else entirely, with creativity encouraged.

The key implication is that Corti did **not** scope the challenge to ambient documentation alone.

## 2. Product/API requirement

The speaker names the following five product areas:

1. dictation;
2. speech-to-text;
3. ambient speech-to-text;
4. text generation;
5. medical coding.

The briefing says teams should use **at least four of the five**.

The speaker also names Corti's **attentive / agentic framework**. In the supplied wording this appears alongside the product discussion but is also later singled out as its own bonus/superlative area. Until official written material is checked, do not silently count the framework as one of the five required product areas.

## 3. Five judging criteria — source-reported as equally weighted

The briefing states that the project will be judged on five criteria, each weighted **20%**:

1. **Clinical relevance** — is it actually helpful?
2. **Use of Corti's API** — including the requirement to incorporate at least four of the five product areas.
3. **Working prototype** — something the team can actually present/show.
4. **Insight and ambition** — explicitly framed as going beyond a typical ambient scribe.
5. **Crowd voting** — Eurovision-style voting; teams cannot vote for themselves.

This should become a direct validation matrix for the final submission and demo rather than an inferred rubric.

## 4. Bonus / superlative categories

The transcript also identifies bonus/superlative opportunities that are separate from the main five-way judging split:

- best commercial idea;
- best user experience;
- best use of the agentic framework;
- a mystery superlative.

The commercial-idea bonus was described as carrying additional founder mentorship and potential investor introductions.

## 5. Critical correction: presentation format

The spoken briefing says the final presentation is approximately:

> **a five-minute demo, followed by a few minutes of questions from three judges.**

Each team member must quickly introduce themselves.

This **supersedes earlier working notes that assumed an approximately 15-minute presentation**, unless a later official source explicitly changes the format.

This changes presentation architecture materially: the final experience must be much more compressed, demo-forward, and immediately legible.

## 6. Demo-media allowance

The briefing says teams may **preload audio components** so presentation time is not wasted, but must still actually show that the system works.

This supports deterministic synthetic/preloaded audio as a presentation technique, while preserving the requirement for a genuine working demonstration.

## 7. Strong wording to preserve

The briefing explicitly says the team should go **“beyond just a typical ambient scribe”** and that Corti wants teams to put its agents/framework to the test and show what can be built.

This is stronger grounding for the team's recurring “above and beyond” language than the earlier memory-only version.

However, “beyond ambient scribe” does not itself dictate which feature or architecture must become the final spine. It is a judging/ambition constraint, not permission for uncontrolled feature growth.

## 8. Candidate interpretation from the prior ChatGPT conversation — NOT assignment truth

The prior ChatGPT response proposed an “open-loop / continuity agent” concept: use conversation-derived clinical understanding to identify care actions that remain unresolved and help close those loops across clinicians/systems.

That is a potentially relevant interpretation of the brief, especially because Corti explicitly mentioned downstream tasks, handoff, longitudinal context, and system integrations.

It is **not** the assignment itself and must not displace the acute/pre-hospital project the team actually built.

## 9. Current presentation validation questions

As actual implementation evidence arrives, the project should be checked against the real rubric:

- **Clinical relevance:** what concrete prehospital/acute-care problem does the demo visibly improve?
- **API breadth:** which four-or-more Corti product areas are truly exercised, and where is the evidence?
- **Prototype:** what can run in front of judges in five minutes?
- **Insight + ambition:** what clearly takes the work beyond an ordinary ambient scribe without pretending roadmap is implemented?
- **Crowd comprehension:** can a non-specialist understand the value quickly enough to want to vote for it?

The purpose of these questions is not to reshape the project around a rubric. It is to make the team’s real work legible against the rubric Corti actually stated.
